### 1. Introduction & Objective

This repository contains the presentation and educational material developed for **Task 02 (Phishing Awareness Training)** during my Cyber Security Internship at **CodeAlpha**.

The primary goal of this project is to spread awareness about phishing attacks, social engineering tactics, and practical defense habits for non-technical as well as technical users. The training emphasizes the key principle: **"Stop – Think – Verify"** before clicking links or sharing confidential data.

---

### 2. Topics Covered in the Presentation

The 22-slide training module is structured into the following sections:

1. **What is Phishing?**
   - Definition and core objectives of phishing attacks.
   - What attackers target: login credentials, banking details, personal data, and unauthorized system access.

2. **Types of Phishing Attacks:**
   - **Email Phishing:** Fraudulent mass emails designed to steal credentials.
   - **Spear Phishing:** Highly targeted attacks directed at specific individuals or organizations.
   - **Smishing:** Malicious SMS text messages.
   - **Vishing:** Voice call scams impersonating trusted authorities or IT support.
   - **Clone Phishing:** Duplicating genuine emails and swapping authentic links with malicious ones.

3. **The 5-Step Phishing Process:**
   - Step 1: Create a fake message or clone website.
   - Step 2: Send message via Email, SMS, or social media.
   - Step 3: Manipulate the victim through urgency, fear, or false rewards.
   - Step 4: Steal credentials or financial data.
   - Step 5: Misuse the stolen information for unauthorized access or fraud.

4. **Identifying Red Flags:**
   - **Fake Emails:** Unfamiliar sender addresses, high urgency/panic, suspicious URLs, spelling/grammar errors, unverified attachments.
   - **Fake Websites:** Misspelled domain names (typosquatting), absence of HTTPS, poor layouts, unexpected pop-ups, unrealistic prize offers.

5. **Common Social Engineering Tactics:**
   - Impersonation, Fear & Threats, Urgency, Baiting, Pretexting, and Trust Exploitation.

6. **Real-World Case Studies:**
   - **2016 DNC Spear Phishing:** Fake Google security alerts leading to credential harvesting.
   - **2020 Twitter Hack:** Vishing attacks targeting Twitter employees to compromise admin tools.

7. **Prevention & Best Practices:**
   - Stop – Think – Verify methodology.
   - Verifying email sender addresses.
   - Enabling Two-Factor Authentication (2FA).
   - Using unique, strong passwords.
   - Keeping browsers and antivirus tools updated.
   - Reporting suspected phishing attempts immediately.

### 3. Interactive Audience Quiz (Slido Integration)

To keep the session interactive, an 11-question quiz was incorporated using **Slido**. Below are the questions and correct answers used during the training:

1. **What comes to mind when you hear the word 'Phishing'?**
   - *Answer:* A cyber attack in which attackers trick people into revealing sensitive information.

2. **Which of these is the main goal of a phishing attack?**
   - *Answer:* To steal sensitive information or gain unauthorized access to accounts.

3. **Which of the following is a common type of phishing?**
   - *Answer:* Spear Phishing (also Smishing, Vishing, Clone Phishing).

4. **What is the first step in a typical phishing attack?**
   - *Answer:* Creating a fake message, email, or website.

5. **Which of these is a warning sign of a fake email?**
   - *Answer:* Unknown sender, unusual urgency, or requests for personal passwords.

6. **What should you check before clicking a link in an email?**
   - *Answer:* Check the actual destination URL by hovering over the link and verify the sender domain.

7. **What is social engineering?**
   - *Answer:* The psychological manipulation of people into performing actions or divulging confidential information.

8. **What should you do if you receive a suspicious email attachment?**
   - *Answer:* Do not open or download it; report it to the IT security team.

9. **Which of the following helps protect your online accounts?**
   - *Answer:* Enabling Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) and using strong passwords.

10. **What should you do if you suspect a phishing attack?**
    - *Answer:* Report it immediately to the relevant organization or security team.

11. **Which of these is the safest online practice?**
    - *Answer:* Manually typing known website addresses directly into the browser instead of clicking email links.
